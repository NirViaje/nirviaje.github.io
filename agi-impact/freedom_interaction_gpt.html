Core axis
Negative liberty = “freedom from interference” • Positive liberty = “freedom to actually do / be capable / self-mastery”.
Why capitalism leans negative libertyproperty • contract • limited state
• Private property defines “protected boundaries” • Markets value voluntary exchange & non-interference (process > outcome) • Suspicion of “grand goals” → fear of paternalism/tyranny • Formal equality can ignore real capability (rights without resources)
Libertarians (自由论者)self-ownership • NAP
• Freedom = not being coerced (negative liberty maximalism) • Tax-funded positive rights seen as coerced taking • Often: small gov → minarchism → anarcho-capitalism spectrum • Tension: civil liberties vs private power (corporate “private tyranny”)
Positive-liberty leaning modelswelfare • capability
• Social democracy (Nordics): education/health/security as rights • Republican/participatory traditions: freedom via self-legislation • Developmental state: state capacity builds “capability base”
Where China sits on the spectrumdevelopment • stability
• Strong positive-liberty rhetoric: “development & subsistence first” • More constrained negative-liberty boundaries (collective/stability priority) • Mixed: market choice + strong state role in strategic sectors
AI era pivot: the “Freedom Crisis”jobs • power • data
• If AI displaces labor widely: “no-work” is not “refusal” but “no access” • Negative liberty risks becoming “freedom to starve” • Positive liberty via redistribution (UBI / digital dividend) seems necessary, but: - dependency risks (state as allocator) - meaning risks (loss of dignity, “digital greenhouse”) • New frontier: freedom as “distance from system” → right to be offline / untracked
